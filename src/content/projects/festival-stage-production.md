The set was run as a small product program, with the same documents an engineering build gets.

- **Requirements.** The program team's sketches became a one-page spec: sightlines, performer
  entrances, lighting positions, truck size, door widths, and the load-in window.
- **Modular design.** The set was modelled in SOLIDWORKS as ten 4 ft by 8 ft modules that two
  people can carry and that fit a 26 ft truck in a fixed order.
- **Kit of parts.** Cut lists and CNC nests came straight from the model. Every part left the
  router labelled with its module and position.
- **Build stations.** Work was split into framing, skinning, foam carving, and paint, each with a
  printed work instruction, so a first-time volunteer could be useful within ten minutes.
- **Schedule.** A Gantt chart with the critical path marked went on the workshop wall, and a full
  load-in rehearsal ran the weekend before.
- **Strike and store.** Modules came down in reverse truck order into labelled racks. A debrief
  the following week fed changes into next year's spec.
